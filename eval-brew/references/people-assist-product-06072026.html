People Assist

Enterprise Workforce Experience Product Strategy

Siddhartha Dhamankar | July 2026 | Accenture Internal

People Assist is Accenture's unified enterprise workforce experience product. It is the single access point through which every Accenture workforce persona, from employees to business leaders, accesses services, guidance, and actions across HR, careers, talent operations, and every corporate function that joins the platform. This document sets out the product strategy, architecture, persona model, domain structure, agent landscape, and experience design principles that govern People Assist today and into the future.

Section 1

What Is People Assist?

People Assist is Accenture's Enterprise Workforce Experience Product. It is the single front door through which every Accenture workforce persona, from employees and managers to staffing specialists and business leaders, accesses the services, information, guidance, and actions they need to do their job and manage their career.

Defining the Product

People Assist is not a chatbot. It is not a portal. It is not a Copilot implementation. It is a long-lived enterprise product with its own identity, its own roadmap, and its own governance.

People Assist is built on the Microsoft Copilot Platform, which is the enabling technology. If that technology were to change, People Assist would remain. The experience, the data, the institutional knowledge, and the trust the product carries belong to Accenture.

Behind the People Assist experience sits a network of specialised agents, connected to enterprise systems and data, orchestrated by a layered routing and execution model, and governed by a shared set of platform services. None of that complexity is visible to the user. They interact with People Assist. Everything else operates invisibly on their behalf.

People Assist launched as an HR experience product serving Accenture India employees. That is the starting point, not the definition. It is designed from the ground up to expand across every corporate function, serve every workforce persona, and operate across every geography and language in which Accenture operates.

Section 2

Product Versus Technology Platform

One of the most important strategic distinctions in this document is the difference between People Assist as a product and Microsoft Copilot as a technology platform. These are not the same thing, and conflating them creates real strategic risk.

People Assist Is The Product
It has a brand, a persona strategy, a domain architecture, a roadmap, and a governance model. It is what the workforce interacts with. It is what Accenture invests in, measures, and evolves. It is the asset.
Microsoft Copilot Platform Is The Technology
It provides the agent development framework, channel integrations, orchestration runtime, and enterprise security model. It is the implementation platform, not the product itself.
Corporate Functions Are Capability Providers
HR, IT, Finance, Procurement, and Learning provide the domain knowledge, processes, data, and governance that People Assist surfaces to the workforce. They are contributors to one enterprise experience.
Technology platforms change. Products endure. People Assist is the product.

Why this distinction matters: It governs how the product is positioned, funded, governed, and evolved. Agents are the service delivery mechanism. When the Employee Self-Service Skill Group (Digital HR Partner) answers a leave query or the Careers Skill Group (Digital Career Coach) surfaces a performance review nudge, the agent is performing a service on behalf of People Assist. The workforce does not need to know which agent responded. They experienced People Assist.

Section 3

Guiding Design Principles

The following principles govern every People Assist experience and product decision. They apply equally to the HR domain today and to every domain that joins the product in the future.

The Workforce Comes First
Every design decision starts with the persona and the moment they are in. What do they need? What context do they have? What is the simplest path to the right outcome? Technology capability is never the starting point.
Invisible Complexity
The workforce should never be asked to understand the systems, agents, routing logic, or data sources operating on their behalf. Complexity is real. It belongs behind the experience, not in front of it.
One Experience, Many Channels
People Assist delivers a consistent experience regardless of the channel through which it is accessed. The interaction pattern, the tone, the quality of the response, and the outcomes available do not vary by channel. The channel is a surface. The product is the experience.
Persona-Aware By Default
People Assist knows who the user is, what role they hold, what domain they operate in, and what context is relevant to their current moment. Personalisation is not a feature. It is the baseline.
Proactive, Not Just Reactive
People Assist does not wait to be asked. It monitors events, detects moments that require action or guidance, and proactively surfaces the right nudge, notification, or recommendation to the right person at the right time.
Complete The Task, Not Just The Answer
Where possible, People Assist moves beyond information delivery to task completion. The goal of every interaction is the best possible outcome for the user, not merely a correct response.
Build Once, Serve Many
Every capability built for one domain should be evaluable for reuse across others. Orchestration, shared services, data access, notifications, and governance are enterprise capabilities, not domain-specific builds.
Trust Is Non-Negotiable
People Assist handles sensitive workforce data, makes recommendations that affect careers and assignments, and acts on behalf of people. Every interaction must be explainable, every action traceable, and every data use compliant with Accenture's privacy and security standards.
Section 4

Personas

People Assist is designed for the full spectrum of the Accenture workforce. It is not an employee self-service tool with manager access added on. It is a multi-persona product where each persona receives an experience calibrated to their role, their responsibilities, and their working context.

All personas access People Assist through a single front door. The experience adapts to each role.

4.1 Workforce Personas

These are the individuals who make up the day-to-day Accenture workforce. Their primary needs centre on accessing services, getting answers, managing their career, and completing personal workforce transactions.

Employees
The largest and most diverse persona group. Their needs span HR self-service, policy guidance, career development, learning, benefits, payroll, and more. The employee experience must be simple, fast, and language-aware. It must work equally well for a junior analyst on their first week and a senior manager with fifteen years of institutional knowledge.
People Leads
The HR business partners embedded within Accenture's business units. They need People Assist to handle the volume of routine employee queries so they can focus on complex, sensitive, and strategic people matters. They also use People Assist to surface insights about their workforce and to manage team-level HR actions.
Managers
Managers have dual needs. They are employees themselves, with personal workforce needs, and they are also responsible for their team's performance, development, and wellbeing. People Assist serves both dimensions and equips managers with the information, guidance, and actions to support their teams effectively.
Capability Leads
Manage large communities of practice within Accenture. Their workforce experience needs are more operational, covering supply visibility, role matching, development planning, and community-level workforce analytics. People Assist surfaces the right operational insights and actions without requiring them to navigate multiple systems.
Business Leaders
Need strategic workforce visibility rather than transactional access. People Assist surfaces relevant workforce intelligence, highlights risks and opportunities, and enables leaders to act on workforce decisions quickly and with confidence.

4.2 Workforce Operations Personas

These are the professionals who run the operational processes that underpin Accenture's workforce management. Their needs are more operational and data-intensive, and they require People Assist to function as an intelligent operations tool, not just a self-service experience.

Talent Supply Chain Operators
Responsible for managing the end-to-end flow of talent supply against project demand. They work with large volumes of data across demand pipelines, talent pools, availability windows, and skill profiles. People Assist surfaces real-time supply and demand signals, supports matching decisions, and progressively automates coordination steps that currently consume significant operator time.
Recruitment Operators
Manage candidate pipelines, interview scheduling, offer workflows, and onboarding coordination. They already have access to autonomous interview scheduling capabilities. People Assist extends this by connecting recruitment workflows to broader workforce data and enabling end-to-end visibility across the recruitment lifecycle.
Staffing Specialists
The practitioners who match supply to demand at the individual assignment level. They use machine learning search and match models to identify candidates. People Assist surfaces match outputs, supports communication and coordination workflows, and progressively connects the steps between matching, engagement, and confirmation that currently require manual bridging.
Workforce Planners
Responsible for modelling future workforce demand, analysing skill gaps, and supporting strategic workforce decisions. They need access to integrated data across headcount, skills, project pipelines, and market conditions. People Assist surfaces relevant analytics and enables planners to act on insights without navigating multiple separate systems.

4.3 Future Personas

People Assist is designed to expand beyond HR and workforce operations to serve the full range of corporate function personas at Accenture. These personas are not in scope for current releases but are accounted for in every architectural and governance decision made today.

Future
Finance Personas
Financial policy guidance, expense management, approval workflows, and financial reporting insights relevant to their role.
Future
IT Personas
IT requests, technical guidance, device and access queries, and proactive notifications about system changes or outages.
Future
Procurement Personas
Procurement policy navigation, supplier interactions, purchase order status tracking, and sourcing guidance.

4.4 Persona Experience Principles

Regardless of persona, the following principles govern how People Assist adapts its experience to the individual.

Role-Aware Responses
People Assist knows the user's role and adjusts the depth, format, and scope of its responses accordingly. An employee asking about leave gets a direct, personalised answer. A people lead asking the same question gets the answer plus relevant policy context and any team-level actions they may need to take.
Context-Sensitive Interactions
People Assist uses available context, including the user's current assignment, location, tenure, recent interactions, and active workflows, to make every interaction as relevant as possible. It does not treat each conversation as if it is the first.
Progressive Disclosure
People Assist surfaces the right level of information for the moment. It does not overwhelm the user with everything it knows. It answers the question asked, offers relevant next steps, and goes deeper only when the user needs it to.
Consistent Tone, Calibrated Depth
The People Assist voice is consistent across all personas: clear, confident, and helpful. The depth and complexity of the response varies by persona and context. A business leader gets a strategic summary. A staffing specialist gets operational detail.
Dignity And Sensitivity
People Assist handles sensitive matters, including performance, health, personal circumstances, and career concerns, with care. It knows when a human referral is the right outcome and makes that transition smoothly.
Section 5

Workforce Experience Domains

5.1 Domain Strategy Overview

People Assist organises its capabilities into workforce experience domains. A domain is a coherent cluster of workforce needs served by a dedicated agent ecosystem, connected to the relevant enterprise systems and data sources, and governed by the relevant corporate function. Domains allow People Assist to scale without losing depth. Each domain can grow its own agent portfolio, use case library, and knowledge base independently, while sharing the same underlying platform services, data foundation, and governance model.

Strategic Intent

HR is the first domain deployed. It is not the only domain that will exist. As Accenture onboards additional corporate functions, each will become a domain within People Assist, served through the same front door with the same quality of experience.

5.2 How Domains Are Structured

Every domain within People Assist follows a consistent structure. This consistency ensures that the experience remains coherent for the workforce regardless of which domain they are interacting with, and that new domains can be onboarded without rebuilding the product model from scratch. Each domain defines the experience it delivers, the personas it serves, the agent ecosystem that powers it, and the use cases it supports. Domains share platform services but own their content, their agents, and their domain-specific knowledge.

5.3 Core HR Domain

Experience Overview. The Core HR domain is the primary service delivery channel for Accenture's HR function within People Assist. It covers the full spectrum of HR self-service, from policy queries and information assistance through to task completion, guided transactions, proactive notifications, and intelligent HR guidance. It is the most mature domain in the People Assist portfolio and the reference model for all future domains.

Personas Served. Employees, managers, people leads, and capability leads. Each persona receives an experience calibrated to their HR role and responsibilities.

Agent Ecosystem. The Employee Self-Service Skill Group (Digital HR Partner) is the primary agent for the Core HR domain. It handles information assistance across HR topics, guides users through transactions such as employee data updates and credence letter requests, creates and routes support tickets, captures feedback, manages live agent handoffs, and delivers proactive notifications and nudges for key HR lifecycle events. It uses intent classification to distinguish between Explore, Decide, Learn, and Fix modes, adapting its response pattern accordingly.

Key Use Cases. HR policy and information assistance across L1, L2, and L3 complexity. Employee data update transactions. Credence letter generation. Support ticket creation and routing. Live agent handoff for complex cases. Proactive notifications for HR lifecycle events including probation milestones, visa renewals, performance review deadlines, and benefits enrolment windows.

5.4 Performance & Rewards Domain

Experience Overview. The Performance & Rewards domain delivers personalised performance management guidance, rewards cycle support, and recognition experiences to Accenture employees. It connects workforce members to their performance reviews, compensation statements, bonus and incentive information, and recognition programmes relevant to their role and level. It operates at the intersection of individual achievement and enterprise reward strategy.

Agent Ecosystem. The Careers Skill Group (Digital Career Coach) is the primary agent for the Performance & Rewards domain. It provides performance review guidance, goal-setting conversations, rewards cycle navigation, recognition programme information, and compensation query assistance. It draws on the employee's performance history, current role level, and active reward cycles to personalise every interaction.

Key Use Cases. Performance review cycle guidance and deadline notifications. Goal-setting and mid-year check-in support. Compensation and bonus statement access and explanation. Recognition programme navigation and nominations. Rewards query resolution. Proactive nudges for performance milestones and reward cycle events.

5.5 Talent Supply Chain Domain

Experience Overview. The Talent Supply Chain domain serves the operational workforce management needs of staffing specialists, capability leads, and talent supply chain operators. It surfaces real-time demand and supply signals, supports matching and assignment decisions, and progressively automates the coordination steps that currently require manual bridging between system outputs and human action.

Agent Ecosystem. The Talent Supply Skill Group (Digital Talent Connector) serves both the employee-facing and operational sides of talent mobility. Employees use it to find and apply for roles, manage their availability, and receive assignment-related notifications. Staffing specialists and capability leads use it for demand management, supply matching, role lifecycle management, and workforce analytics.

Key Use Cases. Demand creation and lifecycle management including extension, cancellation, locking, and validation. ML-assisted supply search and match with operator review and action. Role search and application for employees. Assignment notifications and proactive bench and roll-off alerts. Demand and supply reporting and analytics.

5.6 Recruitment Domain

Experience Overview. The Recruitment domain supports the end-to-end recruitment lifecycle within People Assist. It connects candidates, recruitment operators, and hiring managers through intelligent, partially automated workflows that reduce coordination overhead and accelerate time-to-hire. The autonomous interview scheduling capability already in production is the starting point for this domain's expansion.

Key Use Cases. Autonomous interview scheduling and coordination. Candidate pipeline status and updates. Hiring manager guidance and interview preparation support. Recruitment workflow notifications and escalations. Offer and onboarding transition support.

5.7 Learning Domain

Experience Overview. The Learning domain connects the workforce to relevant learning content, development programmes, and skill-building opportunities through People Assist. It makes learning discovery and recommendation a natural part of the workforce experience rather than a separate system to navigate.

Key Use Cases. Personalised learning recommendations based on role, skill gaps, and career path. Course discovery and enrolment. Learning progress tracking. Learning nudges tied to career milestones and development plans. Manager visibility into team learning activity.

5.8 Future Corporate Function Domains

As People Assist expands beyond HR and workforce operations, each corporate function will be onboarded as a new domain. The experience model, agent taxonomy, shared services, and governance framework remain constant. The domain content, agents, and use cases are function-specific.

6.1 Agent Taxonomy

There are two equally valid ways to define an agent within People Assist. The lens you use depends on the problem you are solving. Both are correct and complementary.

Two Lenses, One Truth

Use the Functional lens when designing products, backlogs, personas, and journeys. Use the Technical lens when designing system architecture, platform constructs, and deployment decisions.

Functional View — Product Design
Make a Parallel to a Human

Think of the agentic system the way you think of a human team. A Skill Group is the person. That person has Skills, things they know how to do. Each skill uses Tools to get the job done.

Skill Group → Skill → Tool
Technical View — System Architecture
Agent Hierarchy

Technically, agents are classified by what they control. An Orchestrator routes and coordinates. A Super Agent handles a domain. A Utility Agent executes a single, focused capability.

Orchestrator → Super Agent → Utility Agent
How The Two Views Map To Each Other

Skill  ≡  Super Agent (L2)

Section 7

Enterprise Agent Federation

People Assist does not operate as a closed agent ecosystem. As enterprise platforms such as Workday increasingly deploy their own agentic capabilities, the ability to federate with those agents becomes a core product requirement. Enterprise Agent Federation defines how People Assist integrates with agents from external enterprise systems, extending its reach without replicating logic that already exists elsewhere, and ensuring that employees and operational teams always interact through a single, consistent experience surface.

7.1 Federation Patterns

People Assist supports two distinct patterns for integrating with external agents. The pattern applied in any given integration depends on which system owns the process and how the human interaction should be structured.

Pattern 1
Delegation

People Assist invokes the external agent as a capability provider. The external agent executes a discrete task and returns a result. People Assist owns the conversation and the user relationship throughout. The external agent operates as a background service, invisible to the employee.

PA Orchestrates → Workday Executes
Pattern 2
Wrapping

The external agent owns and drives an end-to-end business process. People Assist provides the human-facing interaction layer. Employees, managers, and case managers engage with the process through People Assist, while the external agent maintains process state and all business logic.

Workday Owns Process → PA Is The Face
Choosing The Right Pattern
Use Delegation When
The external system holds authoritative data or logic that People Assist should not replicate.
The capability is discrete, with a clear input and a clear output.
The employee experience should remain entirely within People Assist.
Use Wrapping When
The external system owns a complete, multi-step process end-to-end.
Multiple personas need to engage with that process.
A consistent, unified interaction experience across all stakeholders is required.

7.2 Workday Integration

Workday is a committed enterprise dependency for People Assist. Workday operates its own agent platform, implementing HR business processes as first-class agentic capabilities. People Assist federates with Workday agents using both patterns, with the choice determined by what Workday owns and how the human interaction needs to be structured.

Delegation Pattern Credence Letter Generation

A credence letter is a formal document confirming an employee's role, tenure, and employment status. It is commonly required for visa applications, bank account openings, housing tenancy, and similar purposes. Generating the letter requires authoritative employment data and adherence to regional legal templates. Both are owned and managed by Workday.

People Assist delegates this capability to the Workday Credence Letter Agent. The employee requests the letter through People Assist and receives it within the same conversation, without any awareness of the system boundary they have crossed.

Wrapping Pattern Exits Process

Workday Sana Enterprise implements the Exits skill, which manages the end-to-end employee offboarding lifecycle. This is a multi-step, multi-stakeholder process covering equipment returns, system access revocation, final pay processing, manager handover, and exit interviews. Workday Sana owns this process entirely, including its state, workflow logic, and data.

People Assist acts as the interaction layer for every person involved in the process:

Departing Employee
Receives guided task checklists, deadline reminders, and status updates through People Assist's conversational and mobile surfaces.
Line Manager
Receives notifications and guided approval steps, and can track the employee's exit progress through People Assist.
HR Case Manager
Monitors process health, resolves exceptions, and communicates with all parties through People Assist's operational interface.
An Important Distinction

Workday Sana Exits is not a tool that People Assist calls. It is a peer system that maintains authoritative process state. People Assist is the face of that process for every human who engages with it, ensuring that all parties experience a single, coherent interface regardless of where the process logic resides.

Section 8

Notifications And Nudges

Most interactions with People Assist are initiated by the workforce. A question is asked, a task is completed, and the conversation ends. Notifications and nudges work in the opposite direction. People Assist monitors events across HR, talent, and operational systems and reaches out to employees, managers, and People Leads when something requires their attention, a deadline is approaching, or an action has not been completed. This proactive capability shifts People Assist from a system that answers questions into one that actively keeps the workforce informed and on track, without requiring them to seek that information themselves.

8.1 Notifications And Actionable Notifications

People Assist communicates with the workforce through two distinct types of proactive outreach. They serve different purposes, trigger different responses, and are measured by different outcomes. Actionable Notifications are also referred to as nudges in day-to-day usage.

Notifications Actionable Notifications
Tell the user something. Encourage the user to do something.
Information-oriented. Action-oriented.
Relevant even when no action is required. Tied to a specific task. Contains a complete, fit-to-purpose experience within the notification. The user acts and submits without leaving the card.
"Your benefits enrollment window opens tomorrow." "You have been identified as an interviewer. Confirm your preferred interview slot."
Focus on awareness. Focus on behavior change, task completion, and seamless in-context execution.
In Product Terms

A notification answers: "What does the user need to know?"

An actionable notification answers: "What behavior do we want the user to take?"

Notification Example

People Assist surfaces a Mental Health Awareness Month message to the workforce through Microsoft Teams. The notification shares resources, information, and support channels. No action is required. The user reads, absorbs, and moves on.

The notification informs. Nothing needs to happen next.

Actionable Notification Example

People Assist identifies an employee as an interviewer based on their skills and DU match to an open requisition. Rather than directing them to a separate system, the notification delivers a complete scheduling experience directly within the Teams card. The interviewer selects and confirms a slot without leaving the conversation.

The full action, including submission, is completed within the notification itself.

Section 9

Layered Architecture

9.1 Architecture Layers

People Assist is composed of six layers. Each layer has a distinct responsibility. Together they deliver the end-to-end workforce experience product.

The six functional layers of People Assist. The Governance Layer spans all others as an embedded control framework.
Experience Layer
The user-facing layer. It encompasses all conversational and graphical interfaces through which workforce personas interact with People Assist. It manages the interaction pattern, the channel rendering, the tone, and the flow of every engagement. It is the layer the workforce sees.
Orchestration Layer
The routing and coordination intelligence of the product. It receives intent signals from the experience layer, determines which domain, capability, or agent is best placed to respond, manages multi-step and multi-agent workflows, and ensures the right response is returned to the experience layer.
Agent Layer
Where the specialised intelligence of People Assist resides. Domain agents, experience agents, and utility agents operate in this layer. They interpret domain-specific intent, access relevant data and systems, execute actions, and return structured outputs to the orchestration layer.
Shared Services Layer
Provides the reusable enterprise capabilities that all agents and domains depend on. This includes enterprise search, identity and access services, notification delivery, workflow execution, ticketing integration, personalisation services, recommendation services, and summarisation. These are built once and shared across the entire product.
Data Layer
The unified data foundation that all agents and services draw from. It encompasses structured workforce data, unstructured knowledge content, vector stores, knowledge graphs, and enterprise taxonomies. It provides the data access patterns, retrieval services, and privacy controls that govern how data is used across the product.
Governance Layer
Provides the oversight, explainability, traceability, evaluation, and responsible AI controls that govern how People Assist operates. It is not a post-launch concern. It is embedded in the product from the outset and applies to every agent and every interaction.

9.2 Orchestration Model

The People Assist orchestration model is hierarchical. It is designed to route intent to the most appropriate capability as efficiently as possible while maintaining the ability to coordinate complex, multi-step, and multi-agent workflows.

People Assist detects which corporate function an intent belongs to and delegates to the appropriate Level 2 orchestrator. Human Resources delegates further to one of three Skill Group Agents at Level 3. Other corporate functions may adopt different agent designs.
Platform Constraint: Microsoft Copilot

The Microsoft Copilot platform supports a maximum of two Connected Agent levels. This means Copilot natively accommodates Level 1 and Level 2 of the People Assist orchestration model. Level 3 capability, such as the Skill Group Agents shown above for Human Resources, must be implemented using the Child Agent feature embedded within each Level 2 agent rather than as additional Connected Agents in the hierarchy.

Microsoft Copilot Agent Construct
How Copilot Studio structures agents, two types of agent relationship, the features available inside each agent, and the hard constraints you must design within.
Connected Agent
A full Copilot Studio agent that connects to another Copilot Studio agent. Each is a standalone agent with its own features. Maximum 2 in any chain.
Child Agent
An embedded feature inside a Copilot Studio agent, not a separate agent. It is isolated to its parent and cannot be reused across agents. Every agent has this feature available.
Copilot Agent Construct, Hierarchy & Features
Features Inside Every Copilot Studio Agent
Topic Flows
Guided conversation paths for specific tasks, e.g. "request a payslip", "check leave balance". Flows built inside one agent cannot be shared with another agent.
Tools
Connectors that let an agent take action, call an API, read from Workday, write to ServiceNow, or trigger a process. Internal to the agent; not shared.
External Service
Connections to third-party services outside the Copilot platform. Microsoft calls this a “Skill”, we keep the term External Service here to avoid confusion with the functional definition of Skills.
Child Agent , embedded, not a separate agent
An embedded feature within the agent that can run its own Topic Flows and Tools. It is isolated to its parent agent, it cannot be reused by another agent and does not count as a Connected Agent.
⚠ Reusability Constraint, Critical Design Implication
Nothing is shared between agents
Topic Flows, Tools, External Services, and Child Agents are all scoped to the agent they are built in. If two agents need the same capability, it must be built twice, once in each agent.
Implication for People Assist design
Shared capabilities (e.g. "employee lookup") must be planned carefully, either centralised in the primary Connected Agent or deliberately duplicated. Agent boundaries should minimise overlap.
Persona alignment
Agents must be scoped to either employee or non-employee personas. Mixing personas within a single agent creates routing ambiguity and trust inconsistency.

9.3 Cross-Agent Collaboration Model

People Assist agents do not operate in isolation. A single user interaction may require multiple agents to collaborate to produce the right response. The cross-agent collaboration model governs how agents communicate, share context, delegate tasks, and return results to each other.

Agents collaborate through structured handoffs managed by the orchestration layer. Context is passed between agents in a standardised format, ensuring that each agent has the information it needs to complete its part of the workflow without requiring the user to repeat themselves. Agents do not expose their collaboration to the user. From the workforce's perspective, every interaction is a single, coherent People Assist experience.

9.4 Multi-Agent Workflow Patterns

People Assist supports multiple workflow patterns depending on the nature of the task and the agents involved.

Sequential Patterns
Execute agent tasks in order, with each step dependent on the output of the previous one. Used for guided transactions where each step builds on the last.
Parallel Patterns
Allow multiple agents to execute simultaneously when tasks are independent, reducing overall response time. Used when multiple data sources need to be queried at once.
Human-in-the-Loop Patterns
Introduce a user confirmation, approval, or input step at a defined point in the workflow before execution continues. Used for any action that is consequential or irreversible.
Event-Driven Patterns
Trigger agent actions in response to external events, such as a system notification, a calendar milestone, or a data change, rather than a user-initiated conversation. Used for proactive notifications and nudges.
Section 10

Experience Design And Omnichannel Strategy

10.1 User Experience Principles

People Assist is designed around eight experience principles that govern every interaction regardless of persona, domain, or channel.

Clarity Over Completeness
The best response is not the most comprehensive one. It is the most useful one for the moment the user is in. People Assist answers the question asked before offering to go deeper.
Confidence With Humility
People Assist is direct and confident in its responses. It acknowledges uncertainty clearly when it exists and knows when to refer the user to a human rather than risk a poor outcome.
Respect For The User's Time
Every interaction is designed to reach a useful outcome in the fewest possible steps. Unnecessary confirmation steps, excessive clarifying questions, and verbose responses are actively avoided.
Consistent Voice
People Assist has a consistent tone across all personas and channels: clear, warm, and professional. It adapts its depth and formality to the persona and context. It does not change its character.
Proactive Without Being Intrusive
People Assist surfaces relevant nudges, notifications, and recommendations proactively. It is calibrated to add value without overwhelming the workforce with unnecessary prompts. Users can adjust their notification preferences.
Action Over Information
Where People Assist can complete a task rather than just describe how to do it, it does. Every interaction is evaluated for whether the next step is to inform the user or to act for them.
Seamless Escalation
When a matter requires a human, People Assist makes the transition smoothly and with full context. The user does not have to re-explain their situation to the next person in the chain.
Accessible To All
People Assist is designed to be usable across all device types, languages, and levels of digital literacy present in the Accenture workforce. Responses are clear. Interfaces are intuitive. No prior knowledge of the system is required.

10.2 Conversational Experience

Conversation is the primary interaction pattern of People Assist. Natural language input is the default. Users express what they need in their own words, in their preferred language, without needing to use specific commands, keywords, or navigation structures.

The conversational experience is powered by intent classification that identifies not just what the user is asking for but what mode of assistance they need. Are they exploring a topic? Making a decision? Learning how to do something? Trying to fix a problem? Each mode calls for a different response pattern, and People Assist adapts accordingly.

Conversations maintain context across turns. The user does not need to repeat themselves within a session. People Assist recalls what has been discussed and builds on it to progress toward the best outcome. Where a conversation extends into a workflow, the conversational interface transitions naturally to guide the user through the required steps without breaking the interaction.

10.3 Graphical User Experience

People Assist complements its conversational interface with rich graphical components that make complex information easier to navigate, decisions easier to make, and actions easier to complete.

Adaptive Cards
The primary graphical component within the Microsoft Teams channel. They present structured information, confirmation prompts, action buttons, and form inputs in a visually clear format optimised for the Teams environment and mobile-compatible.
Visual Components
Summaries, timelines, status indicators, and comparison views are used where data is better understood visually than in prose. These components are rendered within the conversation flow and do not require the user to navigate to a separate interface.
Workflow Components
Guided multi-step experiences that walk the user through a process requiring multiple inputs or decisions. Workflow components maintain progress state, allow the user to move forward and back between steps, and confirm before executing any irreversible action.
Data Visualisations
Charts, tables, and summary dashboards are surfaced within People Assist for operations personas who need to interpret workforce data as part of their work, reducing the need for those users to navigate to separate analytics systems.

10.4 Proactive Engagement Strategy

People Assist is not a purely reactive product. It monitors the workforce data and event landscape continuously, identifies moments where proactive engagement would add value, and surfaces the right nudge, notification, or recommendation to the right person at the right time.

Proactive engagement is governed by a set of triggers: lifecycle events such as onboarding milestones, review periods, and contract renewals; operational events such as assignment changes, demand signals, and availability windows; and behavioural signals such as incomplete transactions, approaching deadlines, and inferred needs based on role and context.

Calibration Principle

The proactive engagement strategy is calibrated to be valuable without being intrusive. People Assist distinguishes between moments that require immediate attention, moments that benefit from a timely nudge, and moments that can be surfaced in a daily or weekly digest. Users can configure their notification preferences within the bounds of what their role requires.

10.5 Omnichannel Delivery

People Assist is a multi-channel product. The same experience, the same agents, and the same quality of response are available across all channels. The channel is a delivery surface. It does not define the product.

Microsoft Teams is the primary channel. All channels deliver the same People Assist experience — the channel is a surface, not the product.
